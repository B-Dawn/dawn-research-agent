Intrusion detection systems (IDS) deployed in UAV ad hoc networks face a critical gap: existing deep
learning detectors output a decision but not whether that decision should be trusted. We propose a
graph-augmented dual-layer AI trust framework that (1) models the flying ad hoc network topology with
a spatio-temporal graph neural network, and (2) adds a decision-credibility monitoring layer that
produces explainable per-decision trust scores. The credibility layer is trained with a calibration
objective and yields human-readable attributions over graph edges, enabling operators to distinguish
genuine zero-day attacks from distribution shift in low-altitude autonomous swarms. Experiments on
two UAV swarm datasets show a 6.4 point F1 gain over the strongest graph baseline and a 41 percent
reduction in false alarms under topology churn, with only 3.2 ms additional latency on embedded
hardware. We further release an open-source implementation and a benchmark dataset for UAV swarm
intrusion detection under mobility-induced distribution shift.
